## Slice 4: the first UI, and the gate it exposed

`draw_prompt` is procedural, not the three baked keycaps the plan called for — nobody is
authoring that art, and a script-generated PNG plus a bake step is the same machine drawing with
~900 bytes of blob in front of it. Sized against the bitmap font's legible 10×14 after the first
version (a 10 px cap, 4×5 px glyph) read as a dark speck.

Building it exposed a real gate hole: `PORTAL_REACH` (25.5 px) exceeds one tile step (24 px), so
the interact fired from the tile *next to* the portal, and a Kindle-gated portal could be crossed
from the ungated ground beside it. `--gating-test` couldn't see this — it compares walk-reachable
against graph-reachable, and both modelled the portal as an edge crossed by *standing on* an end,
which is stricter than the real interact. Two things that agree with each other can both disagree
with the game. `try_portal` now requires the end be standable.

Task 9 put 4 fragments and 2 Souls past the portal, with the quota counted on tile rows (not
regions — a region can straddle both sectors) and applied inside the reachability filter, never
instead of it. `--play-test` crossed the portal on all 50 seeds for the first time — four
handovers had carried "completability across the portal is untested" as a known gap.

+1,024 bytes for task 8, +0 for task 9. Committed as `50e8427`, `3d03421`.

## Slice 5: dream shards and the Dream Well

8 shards, own array, feed a fixed landmark beside the portal's dream end. Feeding
`SHARD_REQUIRED` (6 of 8) unlocks the Well's own Found Soul — whether she's redeemable is a pure
function of `shards_held`, not a stored lock bit, so there's nothing to keep in sync.
`shards_sufficient()` sits beside `entities_split_ok` in the same generate-then-verify loop, same
reasoning: out of `world_solvable`, never re-checked by the ungating fallback.

The autopilot needed real changes, not just a new interact call: it now searches shards and
entities in one combined nearest-target loop, and excludes the locked well-soul from targeting
entirely — without that it would walk to her, find `try_restore` refuses, and retarget her again
next tick. The livelock shape decision 29 already named, just with a lock instead of a deadband.
Caught by reasoning from the existing pattern before it ever ran, not by watching `--play-test`
hang.

**Two placement bugs, both found by looking, neither by a test:**

- The Well's first ring-search radius (2 tiles) put it inside the portal arch's own silhouette —
  at this projection, 2 tiles diagonal is about one arch-height of screen distance. A screenshot of
  "every Well stage" showed only a portal. No test caught it because none had asserted the
  *distance*; `--shard-test` correctly says the Well and its Soul are co-located, which stayed
  true throughout. Fixed: the search now starts at radius 4.
- The first screenshot rig (`--shards N`) stood the player directly on the Well's own
  ground-contact anchor, and her sprite occluded the (small, dim) basin completely. Fixed: both
  `--shards` and the later `--shard-at` stand two tiles off.

`--play-test` is now 50/50 with the *whole* loop exercised: portal crossings, shard collection,
the threshold crossed, the once-locked Soul walked to and restored — by the same autopilot that
plays everything else. `attempts 1` on all 50 seeds in `--reach-test`: the quota is met on the
first generation attempt, not by falling back to a retry.

+6,144 bytes (768,000 → 774,144). Render went 1.01 → **1.14 ms mean** — reported honestly as an
increase rather than claimed as "no regression"; still 58.9 fps against the 21.333 ms budget, not
attributed to a specific cause (candidates: the Well's per-band draw, the shard loop's
`shard_in_reach` scan, 16 more sprite records).

**Not resolved, and worth saying plainly:** shard pickups were not visually distinguishable from
ambient `PROP_CRYSTAL` decoration. A dozen seeds were swept hunting for one in a static
screenshot; none was identifiable against the dream forest's own scattered crystal spikes, which
share the tapering silhouette and a similar cyan palette family. The mechanism — placement,
collection, the autopilot finding all 8 — is fully proven. The legibility is not, and this is a
real risk to the collect-8-find-6 loop reading as searching rather than wandering. Needs a human's
eye and probably a palette or size pass, not another test.

Committed as `[pending — see git log]`.
