## Slice 1 — Region graph

### Change

The world is now a region graph, per [[World Generation]]. Regions are produced by
farthest-point sampling followed by a multi-source BFS: repeatedly take the walkable tile
furthest **in path distance** from every seed chosen so far, then grow all regions outward
together.

Path distance rather than straight-line distance matters — two tiles either side of a wall are
close in space but far apart to walk, and sampling on straight-line distance produces regions
that straddle walls. Growing outward in lockstep also makes every region connected *by
construction*, so there is no way to get an island of tiles assigned to a region they cannot
walk to.

Terrain types map onto [[Abilities]]: normal, water (Wade), ledge (Climb), dark (Kindle).
Assignment is biased by graph depth from spawn, so gating ramps outward instead of putting a
wall in the player's face on turn one.

### The bug my tests did not catch

All 20 seeds passed the first structural run. Two results did not fit: seed 12 produced **5
regions** and seed 16 produced **8**, despite seed 12 having ~1585 walkable tiles.

Cause: in the previous session's refactor, `seen` changed from an array to a pointer, so
`sizeof(seen)` silently became **8 bytes instead of 3600**. The `memset` cleared almost nothing,
pass 2's flood returned immediately, and the centroid search then ranged over *every* component
instead of the largest one — dropping spawns into tiny side pockets. Seed 12 spawned in a 5-tile
pocket, so the partition had 5 regions.

**Every structural test passed anyway**, and that is the part worth keeping. All of them were
*relative* checks — tile counts agreeing with recorded counts, adjacency agreeing with itself,
each region contiguous. A partition covering 5 of 1585 walkable tiles satisfies all of those
trivially. The check that was missing was the **absolute** one:

> every tile the player can walk to must belong to a region

Added as check 5. It fails loudly on the old behaviour and passes now. Lesson recorded because it
generalises: internal consistency is not correctness, and a suite made only of relative
assertions can be fully green on badly broken output.

## Slice 2 — Reachability invariant

### Change

14 fragments + 5 Found Souls ([[Fragments]], [[Found Souls]] defaults) placed by frontier: each
of the three ability grants goes inside what is reachable *before* it is granted, then the rest
scatter anywhere reachable once all abilities are held.

The invariant itself is checked by simulating an actual playthrough — repeatedly restore
everything currently reachable, bank any abilities that grants, and see whether the frontier
opens further. If the loop stalls with entities left, the world has a dead end and is
regenerated.

Frontier placement only makes solvable layouts *likely*; `world_solvable` is what makes them
*certain*. When 64 attempts fail, gating is relaxed outward from spawn until the world is
solvable — [[Cut List]] lists the reachability guarantee as never cuttable, so losing some gating
is the correct trade against shipping a seed that cannot be completed.

### A test that proved nothing until it did

All 50 seeds passed on the **first** attempt, with gating never relaxed. Good news about the
generator — but it says nothing about the verifier. A checker hardwired to return "solvable"
would produce byte-identical output.

So I added a negative control that builds deliberately unwinnable worlds:

| Case | Expected | Result |
|---|---|---|
| All regions ungated (positive control) | solvable | solvable |
| Every region sealed behind Kindle, Kindle grant sealed inside | unsolvable, 0 reachable | rejected |
| One gate whose own granting fragment sits behind it | unsolvable, 18 of 19 reachable | rejected |

Only with that in place does "50/50 solvable" mean anything.

### Verified — 50 seeds, all PASS

- **19/19 entities reachable on every seed**, first attempt, gating never relaxed.
- Every entity sits on a walkable tile, in the region it claims.
- Exactly 3 ability grants and exactly 5 Found Souls per world.
- Negative control passes.

## Slice 3 — Debug overlay

[[World Generation]] asks for this early, and it earned its place immediately — the grid view is
how a biased generator becomes obvious at a glance rather than after walking one world at a time.

- **F1** — region/terrain overlay, fog off, entities always shown
- **F2** — grid view, 12 seeds at once with terrain, entities and spawn markers
- **R** — regenerate with the next seed
- Window title carries seed / region count / entity count / generation attempts. No bitmap font
  exists until Week 5 ([[Save and UI]]), and the title bar costs no glyph data.

Two defects found by looking at the output: the overlay left **rock still fogged** (the
fog override sat inside the non-solid branch), making region shape unreadable; and grid
thumbnails were stranded in the corner of their cells. Both fixed.

Grid regenerates only when dirty — 12 worlds per frame would crawl — but always re-presents, so a
repaint after surface invalidation cannot leave a blank window.

## NOT verified

- **Pacing.** Whether 16 regions and 19 entities is the right *amount of game* is unknown — there
  is no restoration interaction yet, so nothing can be played end to end. Both counts are
  provisional defaults recorded in [[Open Decisions]].
- **Whether the gating reads as intentional.** The generator produces solvable ability ramps; that
  they *feel* like a designed progression rather than scattered locked doors is a judgement call
  needing a human.
- **The relaxation fallback has never actually run** (0 of 50 seeds). Its code path is therefore
  untested against real failure — only the negative control exercises unsolvable worlds, and it
  does not route through the fallback.
- **Generation time not profiled.** Noticeable when grid view regenerates 12 worlds, but not
  measured.
- Restoration, ability acquisition and the reveal-on-restore coupling are **Week 3**; entities
  currently only draw.
- Still only this development machine.
