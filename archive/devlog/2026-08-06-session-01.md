Tasks 6 and 7 of [[Phase 12 - Dream Realm Plan]]. Slices 1 and 2 had built a second landmass and a

## What landed

**+9,728 bytes for the slice** (756,736 → 766,464), then **+512 for the fix** (766,976). Render
went 1.016 → 0.974 ms mean — a bigger world with more sprites in it cost nothing, because the band
sweep still only draws what is on screen.

### The biome is a palette, not new art

`ArtSprite` keeps `pal_off` into a shared `ART_PAL[]` separately from `data_off` into the shared
pixel stream, so a dream tree is **the same pixel data with a different palette**. Eleven dream
props cost about **600 bytes of palette plus 176 bytes of records**, against ~19 KB to re-author
them. That was the plan's central bet and it paid.

`dream_shift()` in `src/main.c` is the one definition of what the biome looks like;
`ConvertTo-DreamColour` in `tools/bake.ps1` is the copy that has to exist because a palette is
recoloured at *bake* time. Two implementations of one formula in two languages is exactly the drift
the key-magenta list already got caught by, so `--sprite-test` now checks **every** baked `_DREAM`
entry against the C function. They agree **within 1** — PowerShell's `[int]` rounds half to even
where C rounds half away from zero, and that is the whole of the tolerance.

It is a function of **luminance alone**, and that is load-bearing rather than lazy: every output
channel is monotone in the input, so a recolour *cannot* reshuffle which of two colours is
lighter — which is precisely the property `--fog-test` proves `fog_lerp` has. An RGB hue rotation
offers no such guarantee and could silently invert a canopy ramp.

### Two things the checkers now assert that they did not

- **The dream sprites share their twins' pixel streams.** The "nearly free" argument rests entirely
  on this, it was stated in three design documents, and it was checked nowhere. If the emitter ever
  duplicated the stream instead, the art would look identical and the header would quietly double.
- **The value hierarchy holds per sector.** Decision 42 is not a fact about the overworld's
  palette, it is a fact about how a raised surface reads against the ground it stands in. Measured:
  dream ground 52, dream stone **49**, void **38**.

The void ramp is **authored rather than derived**, and `--fog-test`'s new control says why: a
mechanical `dream_shift` of the sea ramp — which is what the plan specified — lands at luminance
**62** against dream ground at 52. A gulf brighter than the land floating in it is decision 42's
pale-floating-cube fault wearing a new costume. The rejected value in that control is one that was
really on the table, not an invented bad number.

### The starfield was free

`tile_detail` already scatters marks from the tile hash, so the void's stars are the sea's speckle
with a pale colour and a 1 px width. It is the one thing in the dream realm deliberately allowed to
out-value the ground: a star is a point light, and decision 42 is about surfaces.

### The portal

`ART_BLD_PORTAL_ARCH` turned out to be **already baked and drawn by nothing** — the team delivered
it with the buildings back in [[Phase 07 - Asset Seam]] and no table ever referenced it. It has been
dead weight ever since. It is now the portal's structure, with eight `fx_portal` frames turning in
its opening, so task 7 cost frames rather than a new sprite.

## Three plan corrections, all found by building it

- **The void band is not `dream_sector`.** Task 1's comment assumed rows 60–63 would take the dream
  palette "by the same path", but `dream_sector(ty)` is `ty >= 64`. The band would have rendered a
  strip of ordinary sea blue along the horizon of a violet void. Resolved with a second, adjacent,
  render-only macro rather than by widening `dream_sector`, which would have quietly handed four
  rows to the dream side in `--sector-test`'s counts and `--land-test`'s per-sector bounds.
- **The plan's animation clock would have frozen the portal.** It specified
  `(int)(p.anim * PORTAL_FPS)`, but `p.anim` resets to zero the moment the keys are released so a
  standing player shows frame 0 — correct for a walk cycle, wrong for anything in the world. Added
  `Game.clock`, advanced by `sim_step`, read only by `render`.
- **`fx_well` and `fx_crystal` were not baked**, against the plan's task 7. Nothing draws the Well
  until slice 5 and **nothing in this phase draws `fx_crystal` at all** — ~16 KB with no caller is
  the exact rule that kept the bitmap font at +0 shipping bytes.

## The bug playing it found in about five minutes

**The portal teleported the player onto ground she could not stand on, on 11 of 100 seeds.**
Reported from the screen, on seed 1, minutes after the slice was called done.

`place_portal` runs **before** `regions_build` — it has to, or the region partition never sees the
dream sector at all — so it picks both ends out of `solid` alone and cannot know what terrain they
will be given. The dream end then sits deep in the region graph *by construction*, which is exactly
where `regions_assign_terrain`'s depth bias gates hardest. Arriving in a `TERRAIN_WATER` region with
no Wade, `tile_blocked` refused her tile, `move_axis` correctly rejected every direction, and the
only input that did anything was `E` to go back.

**Nothing in the suite could have caught it**, and that is the part worth keeping:

- `--gating-test` asserts walk-reachable == graph-reachable. Both agree perfectly that a gated
  arrival tile is unenterable — they are *supposed* to.
- `--portal-test`'s shrink measure was 100/100 before and after. **A component you cannot stand in
  is still a component you can reach.**
- `world_solvable` only ever asks about entities, and there are none in the dream sector until
  Task 9.

Fixed at the source: `regions_assign_terrain` now exempts the portal's arrival region exactly as it
already exempts the spawn. **A gate you arrive inside is not a gate, it is a wall behind you.** The
two cheaper options — refusing to travel, or nudging her to a nearby open tile on arrival — were
both rejected as collision logic papering over a generation fault.

Measured after: **0 of 100 seeds land on gated ground**, mean walkable area from the arrival point
739 → 818 tiles. The bound is deliberately **zero-versus-nonzero**: "can she stand up" needs no
threshold and cannot be quietly loosened later. The negative control gates the arrival region by
hand and requires rejection, so the checker is proven against the fault that actually shipped.

`walk_from()` is portal-**blind** and deliberately does not route through `tile_neighbours` — the
second documented exception beside `land_flood`. "Having arrived, where can you go" is answered
wrongly by an adjacency that includes the portal, because stepping back is the one move that does
not count.

## NOT verified — say it plainly

- **Nothing has been seen in motion.** The vortex is eight frames at 8 fps driven by `Game.clock`;
  no run diffs two frames to prove it advances, and nobody has watched it turn.
- **The dream realm's fogged periphery is the overworld's cool grey haze.** `fog_lerp` is one
  global blend and knows nothing about sectors. That is the core mechanic working as designed — the
  overworld looks the same way — but a violet haze on the dream side is a real option nobody has
  looked at on screen.
- **Rocks stand in the void.** `prop_at` puts boulders on any solid tile, ocean included. In the
  overworld those are rocks in the shallows and read fine; over a starfield they read as floating
  debris. May be on-concept for a floating-island biome, may be a fault. Unjudged.
- **4 of 100 seeds still land in a 6-to-30 tile pocket.** Standable, and legitimate gated design —
  the overworld spawn works the same way. Left to Task 9, where the entity quota makes the existing
  verifier reject a landing that opens onto nothing, with **no invented number anywhere**.
- **`--play-test`'s 50/50 still does not exercise travel** — unchanged since slice 2, and still true
  until Task 9 puts fragments across the portal.
- **The user's verdict on the look was "all good for now"**, which discharges the slice 3 gate. It
  is not the same as "this is the final art direction".

## New tool

**`--dream N`**, self-test only, beside `--overlay`, `--tune` and `--grid`. `--dream 0` stands at
the overworld end, `--dream 1` crosses. Without it there is no way to photograph the biome at all:
the player provably spawns in the overworld on every seed, and the autopilot has no reason to cross
while every entity is still overworld-side. It positions her and calls the **real** `try_portal`, so
a capture cannot show a place the game itself could not put you.
